# Human-Centered Design for AI

This tutorial explains how to apply human-centered design (HCD) to AI systems. It focuses on understanding people’s needs first, checking whether AI is actually the right solution, considering possible harms, prototyping early with users, giving people ways to challenge the system, and building in safety measures. The goal is to create AI systems that are useful, fair, safe, and centered on real human needs.



## Big Idea

- Human-centered design (HCD) means building AI systems around real people’s needs, not just around the technology.
- The main question is not “Can we build it?” but “Should we build it, and how can it truly help people?”


## The 6 HCD Steps

### 1. Understand people’s needs
Observe, interview, and involve the people who actually face the problem. Use their experiences to define the real problem, not just the technical one.

### 2. Check whether AI adds value
Ask if AI is actually better than simpler non-AI solutions. If a rule-based or manual system works well enough, AI may be unnecessary.

### 3. Consider harms early
Think about bias, privacy, misuse, safety, and unintended consequences before building. If harms outweigh benefits, don’t build the system.

### 4. Prototype with non-AI first
Start with a simple mockup or manual version. Test it with diverse users early so you can learn quickly and cheaply.

### 5. Let people challenge the system
Users should be able to question results, report problems, or opt out. This helps make the system more fair and trustworthy.

### 6. Build in safety measures
Test continuously, monitor performance, use human oversight, and prepare for adversarial misuse. Safety is an ongoing process, not a one-time check.
